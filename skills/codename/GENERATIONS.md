# Generations

Aesthetic worlds to name from. Some are eras, some are pure registers — what matters is that
each has its own coherent vocabulary.

**Texture words are raw material, not a shortlist.** They show you how the world *sounds* so you
can find your own word in it. Picking one directly is fine when it is genuinely the right word —
just never let it become the world's default answer, and treat a name already spent as spent.
Every world here holds far more than six words.

**Roman religion & mythology comes first on purpose** — it is the org default. My own repos
already live there: `aurora` the ambient, `vesta` the hearth, `janus` the gateway, `fides` the record,
`lararium` the shrine that holds the household gods. The convention is that the *name's* domain
matches the repo's function — a deity, a cult object, a rite, a festival or a sacred place all
qualify equally. The whole of Roman paganism is in scope; that the first few names happen to be
domestic is a coincidence, not a rule, so do not narrow the world to the household cult. Start
here and leave only when the brief genuinely points elsewhere — and say so when you do, because
the name will read as a stranger next to the family.

**Register** is how the world reads before scope adjusts it.

## Roman religion & mythology

Temple cellae and roadside shrines, cult objects carried out of burning cities, boundary stones nobody may move, oaths sworn and kept, omens read before anything begins.
Texture: palladium, consus, portunus, egeria, sancus, pomerium. Register: formal, durable, load-bearing.

## Deep-sky astronomy

Long exposures, catalogue numbers, patient silence, distances that don't fit on a page.
Texture: albedo, penumbra, perihelion, zodiacal, apsis, occultation. Register: cold, precise, grand.

## Mineral & geological

Deep time, load-bearing rock, cores and strata, things older than everything using them.
Texture: basalt, feldspar, moraine, bedrock, gabbro, olivine. Register: cold, heavy, permanent.

## Liminal & fictional places

Half-real geography, thresholds, places on no map, somewhere you can't quite get back to.
Texture: arcadia, mirage, oasis, xanadu, threshold, elsewhere. Register: dreamlike, weightless to grand.

## Retro computing & broadcast

CRT phosphor, test cards, cassette hiss, dial tones, the handshake of a modem.
Texture: teletext, ceefax, dialtone, bitmap, colorbars, degauss. Register: dated, fond, a bit funny.

## Frutiger Aero / Y2K nature

Brushed glass, bubbles, tropical light, water you can see through, unembarrassed optimism.
Texture: lagoon, cirrus, tidepool, glasswing, seafoam, atoll. Register: warm, light, friendly.

## Synthwave / retrowave

Neon on wet asphalt, night drives, arcade cabinets, chrome sunsets.
Texture: afterglow, chrome, neon, coastline, sunstrip, tailfin. Register: hot, artificial, nocturnal.

## Adding a generation

The list is meant to grow. Add one when a project's context clearly lives somewhere that isn't
here yet — high-alpine mountaineering, brutalist concrete, medieval cartography, deep-sea
bathymetry, analogue photography, whatever the brief points at.

A new generation earns its place when all three hold:

1. It's a coherent sensory world, not a mood word.
2. You can list five texture words without reaching for a single technical term.
3. It isn't a re-skin of one already above.

Write it in the same shape — sensory line, texture, register — so the next call can use it.
